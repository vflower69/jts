Seattle’s Little Wanderer

Jimothy the raccoon isn’t just a Seattle local — he’s a full‑blown cultural phenomenon. Known for his short neck, rounded torso, comically long legs, and no tail, Jimothy first captured public attention when a mid‑July 2026 video of him strolling through Ballard went viral and left the internet collectively asking what kind of raccoon physics they were witnessing.

Long before the memes, Jimothy was already a familiar face to Ballard residents. Locals watched him grow from a small, slightly clumsy youngster into a confident explorer who treated backyard fences like runways. Veterinary experts believe his unique proportions stem from a congenital spinal condition — unusual, but not harmful — giving him the silhouette that launched a thousand fan posts. Seattle embraced him wholeheartedly: the city declared “Jimothy Summer,” murals appeared across Ballard, the National Bobblehead Hall of Fame released an official Jimothy bobblehead.

In one of the most surreal and delightful moments of his rise to fame, the University of Washington officially joined the fun by awarding “Dr. Jimothy” an honorary degree in a widely shared social media post. The announcement — later echoed in local news recaps celebrating his viral stardom — recognized Jimothy for his “contributions to community joy, ecological curiosity, and general raccoon excellence.” Whether Jimothy understood the honor is anyone’s guess, but he accepted it with his trademark calm stare and a slow, dignified stretch.

This Jimothy Tracker was created to celebrate this beloved urban character and help the community understand his habitat. Every sighting contributes to a growing map of his favorite routes, seasonal habits, and interactions with Seattle’s wildlife. Jimothy isn’t just a raccoon — he’s a vibe, a movement, and a tiny‑torsoed ambassador of chaotic good.
